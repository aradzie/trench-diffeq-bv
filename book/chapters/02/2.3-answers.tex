\answer{2.3.20} \part{a}
 The solution is unique on $(0,\infty)$. It is given by
$$
y=
\left\{
\begin{array}{cl}
1,&0<x\le\sqrt{5},\\
1-(x^{2}-5)^{3/2},&\sqrt{5}<x<\infty
\end{array}\right.
$$
\quad \quad \part{b}
$$
\dst y=\left\{
\begin{array}{cl}
 1,&-\infty<x\le\sqrt{5},\\
1-(x^{2}-5)^{3/2},&\sqrt{5}<x<\infty
\end{array}\right.
$$
is a solution of (A) on $(-\infty,\infty)$. If $\alpha\ge0$,
then
$$
y=\left\{
\begin{array}{cl}
 1+(x^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{3/2},&-\infty<x<-\alpha,\\
1,&-\alpha\le x\le\sqrt{5},\\
1-(x^{2}-5)^{3/2},&\sqrt{5}<x<\infty,
\end{array}\right. $$
and
$$
y=\left\{
\begin{array}{cl}
 1-(x^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{3/2},&-\infty<x<-\alpha,\\
1,&-\alpha\le x\le\sqrt{5},\\
1-(x^{2}-5)^{3/2},&\sqrt{5}<x<\infty,
\end{array}\right. $$
are also solutions of (A)  on $(-\infty,\infty)$.
